Introduction

In communities with limited recreational opportunities, and parents with limited financial resources, it is essential that the school promotes physical education and modified junior sport as part of the school curriculum.

By removing the obstacles to participation and shifting mind-set, any smooth surface can become a safe, caring environment for physical activity within schools and communities.

This pragmatic approach ensures every learner has access to the benefits of physical activity, and helps build a lasting love for recreation, health, and sport.

What & Why

What is Fun in Action?

A programme that promotes FUNdamental Movement Skills and life skills through regular participation in modified games. It is age-appropriate and aligned to the physical education curriculum in the Foundation and Intermediate Phase.

Why Fun in Action?

Aligning with school PE outcomes helps more children attain and enjoy optimum health. Success depends on the quality and amount of exposure to fundamental movement skills during the normal school day.

Key Components

1
Early Childhood Development Gross motor skills and core muscle development through play.
2
Promotion of Fundamental Movement Skills Aligned with the Life Skills curriculum.
3
Inter-Class Modified Games Increases participation, limited cost to implement, improves learner discipline.
4
Modified Neighbourhood Leagues Cost-effective to implement, increases participation, builds better communities.
5
Junior Modified Club Sport The natural progression from school-level participation into club sport.

Our Mascot

The team's early idea was a Duolingo-style animated mascot to make the app feel alive for younger players. "Foxy" was the first concept — but the name turned out to already be trademarked, so Mr Dolly gave the team creative liberty to explore a robot direction instead, still built around a sporty, friendly feel.

Concept: Arctic Fox Robot

An early animal-robot hybrid, keeping a nod to the original "Foxy" idea while sitting inside an armoured, robotic body.

Concept: Crane Robot

A tall, bird-inspired build exploring a different silhouette and a sleeker, more mechanical look.

Final Mascot

The friendly, rounded robot design the team settled on — simple enough to animate, and now used across the app and website.
